Which Canadian Province Should You Apply To?

Compare Canada's Provincial Nominee Programs (PNP) by province. Links go directly to the official government page — always confirm current requirements there before applying.

Good to know

If you're more comfortable applying and settling in French, look at New Brunswick — Canada's only officially bilingual province, which runs dedicated Francophone streams and recruitment missions — and Manitoba's francophone pathway. These typically mean lower language-score thresholds and access to targeted draws, not an exemption from the job-offer requirement. New Brunswick's main entry route from abroad (Francophone Workers) still needs a job offer or recruitment-mission offer; its no-offer exceptions (Francophone Priorities, Remote Workers) require already having studied or lived in the province for an extended period first — not a shortcut if you're applying from outside Canada.

Provinces & Territories

Almost every stream across every province requires a job offer from an employer in that province — that's the default to plan around. A handful of narrow exceptions exist (certain graduate, entrepreneur, or in-demand-occupation categories), but which ones are open changes throughout the year. Rather than list specifics that go stale, use the "Official Site" link below for the province you're targeting to see its current streams.

Quebec selects its own immigrants through a separate system, outside the PNP. See the official Quebec immigration site. Nunavut does not run a nominee program.

Common Questions

Can I apply for a PNP without a job offer?

In most streams, no — a job offer from an employer in that province is the default requirement. A small number of exceptions exist (certain graduate, entrepreneur, or in-demand-occupation categories), but which ones are open shifts throughout the year, so check the current streams on your target province's official page rather than relying on a fixed list.

Which province is best for French-speaking applicants from Cameroon?

New Brunswick and Manitoba are worth prioritizing — see the note above. That said, the right province still depends on your occupation, education and where you already have ties.

Do I need to visit the province before applying?

Not usually required, but a visit can help build genuine ties and strengthen your case for provinces that weigh intent to settle.

Can I move to a different province after being nominated?

A provincial nomination is tied to a commitment to live in that province. Relocating soon after landing can raise questions in future dealings with immigration authorities, so it's worth choosing the province you genuinely intend to settle in.

How long does provincial nomination take?

It varies widely by province and stream, and timelines shift often. The official page for your target province will have the current estimate.

Designated Employers

Under the Atlantic Immigration Program, your job offer must come from an employer the province has designated. Here's how it works and where to check.

How It Works

  1. The province designates the employer. Employers in Atlantic Canada apply to their province to be designated before they can recruit through the program.
  2. The employer offers you a job. The offer must come from a designated employer, and you must meet the program's experience, education and language requirements.
  3. The province endorses you. The province reviews your case and, if it approves, gives you an endorsement.
  4. You apply to IRCC for permanent residence. The federal government makes the final decision on your application.

Official Pages

Protect yourself. A designated employer cannot guarantee you permanent residence, and nobody can legitimately sell you a designation or a job offer. Be wary of anyone who asks you to pay to be hired. Confirm that an employer is designated by contacting the province directly through the official pages above.
